Carol's Books — design ideas

A few suggestions from bbbot, the household's assistant, for making Carol's Books simpler and calmer to use. Nothing here has been changed. You pick which ones to do.

"Before" pictures are the app as it is today. "After" pictures are rough sketches, not the finished look.

1

Put your part of the form first

Small change

When you add a book you've read, the things only you know (when you read it, your stars, who recommended it) are halfway down, under boxes the app fills in for you. Moving them to right under the title means you can add a book and save without scrolling.

2

Show the book's details as one tidy summary, with its cover

Medium change

After you pick a title, the app fills in six separate boxes (author, pages, year, category, genre, about). Instead, show them as one small summary with the cover, so you can see at a glance it found the right book. If something's wrong, tap "Change details" to open the boxes. Today the cover doesn't appear in the form at all.

3

Calmer book cards

Medium change

Each book shows up to eight bubbles plus a long description, so one book can fill the whole screen. The dates also aren't labeled ("Oct 12, 2026" — read or recommended?). Instead: title, author, stars, and one quiet line like "Read Oct 12 · from Linda". The description, pages and year stay on the book's own page, one tap away.

4

"Mark as read" asks just two things

Medium change

Tapping "Mark as read" today jumps you to the Read list and opens the whole long form. Instead, a small panel slides up asking only for your stars and the date (already set to today), with a Done button. The button would also say the same words everywhere — today it's "Mark as read" on the card but "Mark read" inside the book.

5

One "Settings" page for backups and devices

Medium change

Back up, Restore, Import from Goodreads and Use on another device are four small underlined links at the very bottom of every list — easy to miss, and easy to tap Restore by accident. Gather them on one Settings page (a small gear by "Carol's Books"), each with a one-line explanation of what it does.

6

Groups: tap a group, see that group

Medium change

Right now tapping a group does different things: a shared group opens its book list, an unshared one opens an edit form, and the "Shared list" button repeats what tapping already does. Instead, tapping any group opens one page with its members, when it meets, its books, and two clear buttons: "Invite friends" and "Edit".

7

One genre, picked from a list

Small change

"Category" and "Genre" are two typing boxes that say "Fills in automatically". Since the genre already tells you fiction or non-fiction, show just one genre button. If the app guessed wrong, tap it and choose from the same list the filter uses — no typing.

8

Fix the buttons at the bottom of the edit screens

Small change

Delete, Mark read and Share link run right to the edges of the screen, unlike everything above them — it looks broken. They'd get the same margins as the rest, and Delete would become a quiet red word set apart from the others so it's never tapped by mistake.

9

A shorter form for friends on the group page

Medium change

Friends who open your group link get the same long form you do, with "Added by (your name)" hidden in the middle. For them, ask only three things: the title (everything else fills in), their name (remembered next time), and "Why read it?". Their book list would use the calmer cards from idea 3, so both pages look alike.

10

A lighter top of the screen

Small change

The search hint is cut off ("Search title, author, pe"), and the "All" box doesn't say what it filters. Shorten the hint to "Search" and rename the box "Genre", so the top of the screen reads at a glance and leaves more room for your books.

Just reply with the numbers you'd like, e.g. "1, 3, 5".Any you don't pick stay exactly as they are.
